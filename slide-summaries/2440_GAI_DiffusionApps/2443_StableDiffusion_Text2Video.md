## Slide 1 — Text2Video
Title slide introducing text-to-video generation as an extension of Stable Diffusion.

## Slide 2 — E2E Video Diffusion Model
Section-header slide that frames the upcoming material on monolithic, end-to-end video diffusion models.

## Slide 3 — Video Generation Challenges
The slide lists two core challenges. Temporal consistency requires the subject to look like itself across frames, and action consistency requires motion to be coherent over time. Inputs are 5D tensors (batch, time, channels, width, height) and the model upgrades the UNet to a UNet3DConditionModel with 3D cross-attention to mix information across the time axis.

## Slide 4 — Example
The slide shows a strip of four sequential frames generated by an end-to-end text-to-video model, demonstrating that subject identity and motion follow the prompt across time.

## Slide 5 — AnimateDiff
Section-header slide that introduces AnimateDiff as an alternative recipe: fine-tune an existing Stable Diffusion model for video rather than train a video diffusion model from scratch.

## Slide 6 — AnimateDiff
AnimateDiff starts from a pretrained SD UNet, formats the video sequence's latents as a batch, and inserts temporal-attention layers across the batch dimension after selected UNet blocks. The new layers learn motion while the spatial blocks keep their pretrained image priors.

## Slide 7 — Example
A four-frame example shows the AnimateDiff output for a single prompt, with the subject and lighting consistent across frames thanks to the inserted temporal attention.

## Slide 8 — Reminder: LoRA
The slide recaps Low-Rank Adaptation as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning backbone that motion LoRAs will reuse.

## Slide 9 — Motion LoRA
Motion LoRAs attach low-rank adapters only to the motion-attention modules, modifying their Q, K, V projections. Each motion LoRA encodes a camera or scene motion such as zoom-in or pan, which can then be invoked as a separate style without retraining the base.

## Slide 10 — Other LoRA adapters
The slide highlights other community LoRA adapters that compose with motion LoRAs, including character LoRAs and style LoRAs, illustrating the modular adapter ecosystem around AnimateDiff.

---

## Deck-level takeaway
Text-to-video diffusion either replaces SD's 2D UNet with a 3D variant (UNet3DConditionModel with 3D attention) for end-to-end training, or augments a frozen 2D UNet with temporal attention modules as in AnimateDiff. The AnimateDiff recipe is especially useful because motion LoRAs (camera motion such as zoom-in) can be trained independently and composed with character and style LoRAs to produce controllable video without retraining the full backbone.
