## Slide 13 — Background: Low-rank matrix
A low-rank matrix can be represented as the product of two low-dimensional matrices. Original elements n*m are replaced by r*(m+n) effective parameters. For m = n = 1000 and r = 5 the reduction is from 1M to 10K.

## Slide 14 — Low-Rank Adaptation: LoRA
LLM parameters are organized in huge matrices (attention Q/K/V, linear feed-forward). LoRA updates each such matrix by adding a delta that is represented as a low-rank matrix.

## Slide 15 — LoRA Training
Only the low-rank parameters in the delta are updated during training; the base matrix is frozen.

## Slide 18 — Background: Floating point number representation
A reminder of floating-point representation: sign, mantissa, and exponent (positive or negative).

## Slide 19 — Model Compression
Quantization reduces the number of bits from 32 to 16 (or fewer).

## Slide 20 — Quantization: Finite Number of Possible Values
Naive uniform quantization partitions the entire range into uniformly-sized bins, which may lose differentiation between similar weights.

## Slide 21 — Blockwise Quantization
Quantize each block of parameters separately using block-level statistics. For example, split a matrix into 16 blocks and use a separate scaling factor for each.

## Slide 22 — Distribution-aware blocks
NN weights are often approximately normally distributed; quantization bins can follow the distribution so that frequent values are represented with higher precision and smaller bins.

## Slide 23 — Double Quantization
Quantize the quantization parameters themselves (scales and zero points for each bin) to save additional memory.

## Slide 24 — QLoRA
Section divider for QLoRA.

## Slide 25 — QLoRA: 4bit quantization +LoRA
QLoRA uses 4-bit quantization (16 possible values) with bins derived under the standard normal assumption. Original model weights are quantized; the LoRA delta is kept full-precision during training. Computations run in fp16/fp32 and convert on the fly. The slide lists the 16 NF4 anchor values used (e.g., -1.0, -0.6961928, ..., 1.0).

## Slide 26 — Merging QLoRA weights
After training, the new weights are merged: original quantized weight + delta weights in float16, then add and re-quantize.

## Deck-level takeaway
The deck is a deep tour of PEFT. It opens with the three pain points (overfitting, catastrophic forgetting, training cost) that motivate freezing most weights, then walks through the two main families: Adapters (small inserted bottleneck modules, supported by AdapterHub) and LoRA (low-rank delta on big projection matrices). It then layers in quantization (uniform, blockwise, distribution-aware, double quantization) and combines the two ideas into QLoRA, where a 4-bit NF4-quantized base model carries a full-precision LoRA delta during training. The closing arc runs two end-to-end pipelines with the same machinery: QLoRA-based supervised instruction-tuning of TinyLlama on UltraChat via SFTTrainer, followed by QLoRA-based DPO preference alignment of that fine-tuned model via DPOTrainer, with the merge-and-save step at each stage.
