## Slide 1 — Course Structure I: Basics
This slide lays out the course operating model through three teal-banded panels stacked vertically. The first panel, "Non-Trivial Course Project," establishes that work is done in teams throughout the course, that weekly tasks require planning, parallel execution, and consistent progress, and that all deliverables live in the project GitHub repository. The second panel, "Course Material," explains that the syllabus covers many topics needed to build a non-trivial project, that theory and code are tightly integrated rather than sequential, and that the course begins by assembling a project toolbox and then revisits and expands concepts later. The third panel, "AI Assistance and Code Reuse," encourages the use of any AI tools or reuse of any library or code, frames the ethos as doing "more with less" by reusing and automating, and reminds participants that they retain full responsibility for the project scope and all produced artifacts. Together the three panels function as the foundational contract of how the course is run.

## Slide 2 — Course Structure II: Novelty
Marked with a red asterisk indicating the rules apply specifically to LLM and GenAI courses, this slide presents three icon-and-text rows that layer additional expectations onto the basics. The "State-of-the-Art Orientation" row (easel icon) asks participants to integrate contemporary tools and emerging ideas and notes that there is no textbook because knowledge evolves rapidly. The "Project Novelty" row (magnifying-glass icon) requires solving a valuable, previously unaddressed problem, conducting thorough research to identify genuine gaps, and accepting that inventing new stuff is tough but unavoidable. The "Presentation and Feedback" row (people icon) requires three presentations, proposal, interim, and final, with iterative refinement based on feedback, and frames clear communication as part of the professional skillset. The footnote "* For LLM/GenAI Courses" scopes these heightened novelty demands to the frontier-topic offerings.

## Deck-level takeaway
The deck compactly codifies the course's pedagogical contract in two halves: a universal "basics" layer covering team-based GitHub-tracked projects, integrated theory-and-code material, and unrestricted AI and code reuse paired with full author responsibility; and a frontier "novelty" layer (LLM and GenAI only) requiring state-of-the-art orientation, genuinely novel project ideas, and a three-presentation cadence with iterative feedback.

The third slide grounds the abstract expectations by pointing to five concrete prior-cohort course sites, giving new participants browsable evidence of what "non-trivial" and "novel" actually look like in practice.
