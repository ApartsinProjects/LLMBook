Exercise 34.10.1: The Tokenizer Is the Theory Conceptual

Section 34.10 argues "the tokenizer is the theory": choosing how to slice your input encodes domain assumptions before training begins. For each domain, name one tokenization choice and the assumption it bakes in: (a) DNA (Nucleotide Transformer's k-mers vs. Evo's single nucleotides); (b) proteins (ESM's amino-acid alphabet); (c) molecules (SMILES vs. SELFIES); (d) time series (Chronos's regression-via-classification quantization).

Answer Sketch

(a) k-mers (k=6) bake in the assumption that 6-base motifs are the relevant biological unit (codons span 3, regulatory motifs ~6-12 bases). Single nucleotides drop this prior and let the model learn motifs end-to-end at the cost of much longer sequences. (b) ESM's 20-amino-acid alphabet treats each residue as atomic, encoding the assumption that biology composes proteins from amino acids and that side-chain chemistry need not be exposed to the model. (c) SMILES tokenizes by character and inherits the language's brittleness: small string changes can break valence rules. SELFIES is grammar-constrained so every string is a valid molecule; the tokenizer choice eliminates an entire class of invalid generations. (d) Chronos quantizes continuous values into a fixed bin vocabulary and reframes forecasting as classification. The assumption: forecast distributions are best modeled multimodally (any bin can win) rather than as single Gaussian regressions, which lets one model handle disparate scales.

Exercise 34.10.2: Cross-Domain Transfer Predictive

Section 34.10 lists DNA, proteins, molecules, time series, audio, EHR, robotics, weather, theorem proving, tabular, finance, and games as transformer-amenable domains. (a) For each, predict the rough order of magnitude of training tokens currently available (small, medium, large). (b) Which domain do you predict will see the next big foundation model breakthrough, and what tokenization advance would make it possible? (c) The "Library Shortcut: BioNeMo" callout points to NVIDIA's biology stack. Why are vendor-specific frameworks emerging instead of one universal one?

Answer Sketch

(a) Large (10B+ tokens): proteins (UniRef has 200M+ sequences), audio (web-scale), weather (decades of reanalysis). Medium (100M-10B): DNA, EHR (PHI-restricted but plentiful at health systems), robotics (Open X-Embodiment ~1M demos). Small (<100M): theorem proving (Mathlib ~150k theorems), finance (regulated, fragmented). (b) Robotics is most likely: Open X-Embodiment scaled to 22 robot embodiments shows transfer is possible, and the next advance is action-tokenization that unifies continuous joint commands across hardware. RT-2 and OpenVLA point this direction. (c) Domain-specific frameworks emerge because the data pipelines (FASTA parsing, PDB structures, EHR FHIR resources) and evaluation harnesses (CASP for proteins, MTEB for retrieval) require deep domain plumbing that a general framework cannot abstract. The transformer block stays the same; everything around it specializes. This is why we see BioNeMo, Chronos, and the AlphaFold ecosystem rather than one monolithic library.

Exercise 34.10.3: Chronos Discretization Tradeoffs Calculation

Chronos discretizes continuous time series into a fixed vocabulary of B bins (B = 4096 in the original paper). (a) For a series with values uniformly distributed in [0, 1] and B = 4096, what is the maximum quantization error per value? (b) Why does Chronos use a per-series scale normalization before quantization? (c) The model emits a categorical distribution over bins at each step. How would you convert this back to a continuous prediction with calibrated uncertainty, and why is that often more useful than a point forecast?

Answer Sketch

(a) Quantization error is bounded by half the bin width: 1 / (2 * 4096) ~ 0.000122. For most forecasting use cases this is far below noise. (b) Real time series span many orders of magnitude (a stock price near 1 vs. a sales figure near 10^6). Without per-series normalization, fixed bins would either waste resolution on the easy series or saturate on the hard one. Chronos rescales each series to a common range before quantization, then de-normalizes at the end. (c) Convert by computing the expected value (sum of bin centers weighted by probabilities) and the variance (or any quantile) over the categorical distribution. This yields full predictive distributions for free, useful for probabilistic forecasting, anomaly thresholds, and downstream decision making (e.g., inventory planning needs the 95th percentile, not the mean). Point forecasts collapse this to a single number and discard the model's uncertainty estimate.

Exercise 34.10.4: Action Tokens for Robotics Failure Mode

Section 34.10.9 frames robotics as treating actions as tokens (RT-2, OpenVLA). (a) Sketch how a 7-DOF arm command might be tokenized. (b) Why does naive per-dimension binning fail when actions need to be coordinated across joints? (c) What is the failure mode at deployment if the tokenizer was trained on data from one robot and you deploy on a slightly different one (different gear ratios or end-effector mass)?

Answer Sketch

(a) Discretize each of the 7 joints into B bins (e.g., B = 256), tokenize an action as 7 tokens; or learn a vector quantizer (VQ-VAE) that maps the 7-dim action to a single discrete code from a learned codebook of K entries. RT-2 uses the simple per-dim binning; newer work prefers VQ for compression. (b) Per-dim binning treats joint angles as independent, but real motions are constrained: closing a gripper while moving the wrist is a coordinated trajectory whose joint distribution looks nothing like the marginal product. The model wastes capacity learning to avoid joint combinations the data already excludes. (c) The token vocabulary is calibrated to one robot's kinematics. Deploying on a different robot makes tokens map to different physical motions, and the model's "press button" action becomes "smash button". Mitigations: action normalization to a robot-agnostic frame (end-effector pose space rather than joint space), embodiment tokens that condition on robot ID, or the Open X-Embodiment recipe of training on many robots so the model learns a shared abstraction.
