Models

Section 30.4

Agentic workloads put unusual pressure on models. They need strong function calling, instruction following over long traces, ability to recover from errors, and (for browser/code-use agents) multimodal grounding. The model recommendations differ from a generic chat workload.

30.4.1 Frontier models for agents (2026)

  • Claude Opus 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5: dominant on SWE-bench and most agentic benchmarks; Anthropic's tool-use API is the most reliable.
  • GPT-5 and the o-series reasoning models: strong on tau-bench and on agentic workflows that benefit from reasoning before acting.
  • Gemini 2.5 Pro: long-context strength makes it the choice for agents that operate over very large state.
  • Grok 4: competitive on browsing-style benchmarks because of X integration.

30.4.2 Open-weights options

  • Llama-4 Instruct: usable for agents at 70B; struggles below 8B on multi-step traces.
  • Qwen3-Coder: code-specialized variants of Qwen3; competitive on SWE-bench.
  • DeepSeek-V4: cost-efficient frontier; popular in self-hosted agent stacks.
  • DeepCoder and similar coding-RL fine-tunes: smaller but task-specialized.

30.4.3 Vision-language models for browser agents

Browser-using agents need to read screenshots, not just DOM. The relevant models:

  • Claude with Computer Use: Anthropic's screen-reading agent capability.
  • ShowUI: open vision-language model for UI grounding.
  • Various open VLMs (Qwen-VL, Llama-Vision).

Tool-call accuracy is the metric

Most agent failures trace back to a single bad tool call: wrong arguments, missing field, wrong tool entirely. The model's reliability on the function-calling API is more predictive of agent success than its raw IQ. Claude and GPT-5 lead this metric as of mid-2026; open-weights models trail by 5 to 20 points.
