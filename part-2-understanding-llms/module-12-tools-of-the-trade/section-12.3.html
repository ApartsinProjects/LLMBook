Datasets & Benchmarks

Section 12.3

Part II is where the dataset list gets serious. Pretraining (Chapter 7), reasoning evaluation (Chapter 9), and inference benchmarks (Chapter 10) all draw on a different shelf of corpora. The lists below name the dataset, its rough size, and what part of the book uses it.

12.3.1 Pretraining corpora

  • FineWeb: ~15T tokens of cleaned web data released by Hugging Face in 2024. The current open standard for pretraining English LLMs at scale, with documented filtering and a public quality-classifier pipeline (FineWeb-Edu).
  • Common Crawl: the raw web archive that FineWeb, C4, and most other web corpora derive from. Petabyte-scale; you process it with Spark or RedPajama-Data's pipeline.
  • RedPajama-1T: the open replication of the Llama 1 training data mix. Useful for studying the recipe rather than for training new frontier models.
  • The Pile (EleutherAI): 825 GB across 22 sources, the standard "diverse text" corpus for academic comparison. Largely superseded by FineWeb for new runs but still cited for reproducibility.
  • The Stack v2: code corpus from BigCode, used to train StarCoder-2 and similar code models.

12.3.2 Reasoning and evaluation benchmarks

Chapter 9 (test-time compute, reasoning) leans on a different shelf:

  • MATH: competition math problems with worked solutions; the canonical math-reasoning benchmark.
  • GSM8K: 8,500 grade-school word problems, much easier than MATH but still discriminating between reasoning approaches.
  • MMLU and MMLU-Pro: 57-domain multiple-choice knowledge benchmarks; saturated by frontier models but still useful as a regression test.
  • GPQA: graduate-level science questions, designed to be Google-proof.
  • HumanEval and MBPP: code-completion benchmarks.
  • Chatbot Arena (LMArena): human-preference Elo leaderboard; the closest the field has to a vibes-check at scale.

12.3.4 Saturation and data leakage

Most pre-2024 reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, parts of MMLU) are at or near saturation for frontier models. The 2026 evaluation conversation has shifted to ARC-AGI, AIME, and live arenas. Treat the older benchmarks as regression tests, not as scoreboards. Appendix N has the master comparison table updated for 2026.

Leakage is real

Any benchmark released before a model's training cutoff is potentially in the training data. Always check whether a published score was on a held-out test set or on a contaminated public split before comparing models.
