Platforms

Section 12.1

Part II's chapters live on top of three platform layers: the cloud GPU/TPU services where the actual training and inference run, the model-hosting platforms where the weights live, and the inference-orchestration layers that turn checkpoints into latency-bounded services. None of these are libraries you import directly. They are accounts you create and quotas you negotiate.

12.1.1 Cloud GPU and TPU providers

For pretraining at the scales discussed in Chapter 7, you need cloud compute. As of 2026 the practical options are AWS SageMaker, Google Vertex AI, Azure Machine Learning, and the GPU-rental specialists Lambda, CoreWeave, RunPod, and Vast.ai. The hyperscalers offer integrated dataset and experiment tooling but lock you into their ecosystem; the specialists offer better hourly rates on H100s and H200s but you bring your own orchestration.

Google's TPU pods remain the cheapest option for very large runs if your training code is in JAX; the v5p and v6e (Trillium) pods are what trained Gemini 2.5 and most of the public Gemma checkpoints. For most Part II readers, a single 8xH100 node rented by the hour on Lambda or RunPod is the right starting unit. Appendix I covers the VRAM math for sizing these runs.

12.1.2 Model hosting and registries

The Hugging Face Hub remains the de facto registry for open-weights models. GitHub hosts the training code; Weights & Biases or MLflow tracks runs. For private hosting, Hugging Face Enterprise and the cloud-native registries (SageMaker Model Registry, Vertex Model Garden) all work; pick whichever your team is already invoiced through.

12.1.3 Frontier API providers

The closed-weights frontier providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google AI Studio / Vertex AI, xAI) are technically platforms, not libraries. Their pricing, rate limits, and feature parity move quarterly. Chapter 16 covers using them properly; for now, register accounts, save API keys to a password manager, and never commit them to GitHub.

12.1.4 Comparing the platforms

12.1.5 Choosing a platform

For one-off experiments (Chapter 11 interpretability runs, small Chapter 9 reasoning evaluations), rent an H100 by the hour on Lambda or RunPod. For continuous training that runs for days, the hyperscalers' reservation pricing wins. For pretraining experiments larger than a few hundred GPU-hours, talk to your CFO before opening a terminal.

Hygiene

Always set a billing alert before launching a multi-day job. Spot instances can vanish; on-demand instances can vanish your budget. The same lesson recurs in Part VIII when we discuss production cost monitoring.
