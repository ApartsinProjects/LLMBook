World Models: Video Generation, Simulation, and Embodied Reasoning

Section 32.4

A world model is a system that predicts what comes next given what just happened. Frame the input as text and the loss as cross-entropy and you have a language model. Frame the input as video frames plus an action and the loss as pixel reconstruction (or representation prediction) and you have a world model. The conceptual collapse, "next-frame is next-token", is what lets 2026 frontier labs reuse essentially all of the transformer-training infrastructure built for LLMs to train world simulators that an agent can roll out as if they were physics.

This section walks through the three families of world models that emerged in 2024 and 2025: pixel-level interactive generators (Genie 3), self-supervised representation predictors (V-JEPA 2), and domain-specific simulators (GAIA-2 for driving). We close on the most consequential point: world-model rollouts can serve as chain-of-thought for an embodied agent, the visual analog of the textual reasoning chains from earlier in the book.

32.4.1 The next-frame as next-token analogy

The training objective for a pretraining LLM is "given tokens 1 to t, predict token t+1". The training objective for a video world model is "given frames 1 to t (and optionally an action), predict frame t+1". The architectures look nearly identical: a transformer over a flattened sequence of patches, a causal mask, an autoregressive head. The differences are practical: video uses many more tokens per "word" (a 256x256 frame is hundreds of patch tokens), so attention complexity dominates and most production systems use latent-space prediction over a learned VQ-VAE or VAE codebook. The conceptual move is the same as Section 6.2's pretraining objectives: pick a maximally information-dense self-supervised loss, scale data, scale parameters, scale compute. The model picks up world physics as a side effect of trying to compress video.

Next-frame is next-token

Every architectural advance from LLMs (RoPE scaling, mixture-of-experts, FlashAttention, speculative decoding) transferred to world models with minimal modification. The reason world-model labs are LLM labs in different t-shirts is that the math is the same; only the modality of the tokens differs.

32.4.2 Genie 3: interactive worlds from a prompt

Genie 3 (DeepMind, August 2025) is the cleanest demonstration of the "interactive world from a single prompt" capability. Given a text prompt ("a Victorian-era stone courtyard with falling autumn leaves"), Genie 3 generates a coherent 3D environment that an agent (or a human with WASD keys) can navigate in real time at 24 FPS and 720p resolution. Memory persists for minutes of play: walk away from a tree, come back, the same tree is there. The architectural details are partial (the paper does not fully release weights), but the public summary describes a frame-causal latent transformer with a memory module, trained on a curated mix of game footage, real-world video, and procedurally generated environments.

Why Genie 3 matters beyond demos: it is the first world model where the world is good enough for an agent to do useful skill learning inside it. Earlier world-model RL methods (DreamerV3 family) trained agents on tiny imagined rollouts; Genie 3 raises the ceiling toward rolling out an agent through a synthesized environment indistinguishable from a low-budget video game.

32.4.3 V-JEPA 2: representation prediction, not pixel prediction

V-JEPA 2 (Meta, 2024) takes a different stance. Rather than predict pixels of the next frame, V-JEPA 2 predicts the representation a separate encoder would produce for the next frame. The motivation, repeatedly argued by Yann LeCun, is that pixels contain enormous amounts of information that does not matter for planning (the exact texture of a leaf, the precise specular highlight on a window). Predicting in representation space lets the model concentrate capacity on the bits that change with action and time. V-JEPA 2 reached state-of-the-art on a battery of video-understanding benchmarks while using a fraction of the compute of pixel-predictive baselines, and the representations transfer cleanly to downstream embodied control tasks.

32.4.4 GAIA-2: a domain-specific world model

GAIA-2 (Wayve, 2025) is the autonomous-driving incarnation: a video-prediction model trained on hundreds of thousands of hours of driving footage, conditioned on vehicle controls (steering, throttle) and traffic rules. The product is a simulator: drop your driving policy into GAIA-2 and the model rolls out plausible future scenes including reactions of other agents to the policy's actions. The 2025 deployment story is that Wayve uses GAIA-2 rollouts as a complement to real-world testing, generating millions of synthetic miles per day to stress-test policy edge cases that would take years to collect on the road. The result is roughly an order of magnitude improvement in evaluation throughput.

32.4.6 World-model rollouts as visual chain of thought

The most consequential framing: a world-model rollout is chain-of-thought for an embodied agent. When a language model thinks step-by-step, it generates intermediate tokens that condition the final answer. When an embodied agent thinks step-by-step, it can roll out the world model under candidate actions and pick the trajectory whose predicted outcome best matches the goal. The agent never has to physically try the bad actions; it imagines them, evaluates them, and acts only on the survivors. This is mathematically just Monte Carlo Tree Search with a learned simulator, but the language framing matters: it lets the same engineers who built reasoning models for text reuse their intuitions for action.

# Pseudocode for a world-model planner (one timestep)
candidate_actions = sample_actions(policy, current_obs, n=32)
imagined_rollouts = [world_model.rollout(current_obs, a, horizon=20)
                     for a in candidate_actions]
scores = [score_fn(rollout, goal) for rollout in imagined_rollouts]
best_action = candidate_actions[argmax(scores)]
robot.execute(best_action)  # only the best is actually performed
Training agents inside synthesized rollouts

The 2025 production pattern: pretrain a world model on real video; train a policy by reinforcement learning inside the world model; finetune the policy on real hardware for a small number of episodes. The dominant cost is the world-model pretraining (millions of GPU hours, once); the marginal cost per new policy is small. DeepMind's DreamerV3 lineage and Wayve's GAIA-2 are the published references; most frontier robotics labs run a private variant.

32.4.7 The honest limitations

World models hallucinate physics. Genie 3 occasionally produces frames where gravity reverses, where an object passes through a wall, where a tree multiplies. V-JEPA 2's representation predictions are abstract enough that the loss-of-fidelity is invisible to humans but very visible to a policy that relied on it. GAIA-2 is more constrained because the data is narrow, but it still produces edge-case rollouts that a real driver would never see. The 2026 mitigation pattern is to use real-world calibration loops: run the policy briefly in the real world, measure where world-model predictions diverged from reality, retrain the world model on the freshly observed data. This is the "model evaluation is the product" thesis from Part VIII applied to physics simulation.
