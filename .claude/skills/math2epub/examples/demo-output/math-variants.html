Plain-HTML Math: Three Placement Variants

Six staple expressions rendered in four placement contexts. The goal: verify that nothing breaks mid-formula regardless of where the math appears.

1. Inline math

Math threaded through running prose paragraphs. Each expression is wrapped in <span class="math-inline">, which uses display:inline-block; white-space:nowrap so the entire expression stays together as one atom even if the surrounding line wraps.

y_i. In context: the residual yi captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Simple subscript: italic variable with italic subscript.

x^2. In context: the residual x2 captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Simple superscript: italic variable with superscript exponent.

y_hat_i. In context: the residual yi captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Combination: hat over variable, then subscript.

MSE. In context: the residual MSE = 1n nΣi=1 (yi − yi)2 captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Full MSE expression: operator name, fraction, summation with limits, subtraction, hat, sub, sup.

sigma(z). In context: the residual σ(z) = 11 + e−z captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Sigmoid expression: Greek letter, fraction, exponent with negative.

integral. In context: the residual π∫−π f(x) dx captures what we are measuring, and we usually report it across many examples. Integral with stacked limits: hi/lo float above/below the integral glyph.

4. Stress test: long expression in narrow column

The scaled dot-product attention formula is wider than most prose expressions. In a narrow column, it should still render as one horizontal atom and either fit, or scroll, but never break in the middle of the equation.

Inline: the transformer head computes Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKT√dk) V, which the rest of the architecture composes via residual connections.
