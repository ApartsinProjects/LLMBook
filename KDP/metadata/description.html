Build and Operate Production LLM Systems and Agents from First Principles

A 79-chapter LLM textbook (chapters numbered 0 through 78, opening with a Chapter 0 PyTorch and classical-ML primer) that takes you from PyTorch fundamentals to shipping autonomous agents in production. Over 1,000 pages of theory, working code, and production engineering across 15 parts plus 6 reference appendices, for engineers, researchers, and students who need to build and operate real LLM systems. The Sixteenth Edition adds a new chapter on LLM-powered recommender systems (TIGER, LLaRA, P5), a complete audio and speech track (codecs, wav2vec, HuBERT, Whisper, CLAP), a PyTorch reference appendix, a signal-processing primer for audio, and full architecture diagrams plus math plus code plus worked examples for every load-bearing algorithm.

If you have spent the last year learning LLMs from scattered blog posts and outdated tutorials, this book is for you. Every chapter answers a question someone shipping LLM systems has actually had to answer: how attention works, how to fine-tune a model on your data, how to build an agent that uses tools without going off the rails, how to evaluate what you built, and how to run it reliably at scale.

What you will learn

  • Foundations and understanding (Ch 0-10): PyTorch and ML, NLP, tokenization, attention and the Transformer from scratch, decoding, pretraining and scaling laws, reasoning and test-time compute, inference optimization, and interpretability.
  • Working, training, and adapting (Ch 11-19): provider APIs and structured output, prompt engineering, hybrid ML+LLM systems, synthetic data, fine-tuning, PEFT (LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA), distillation and merging, and alignment (RLHF, DPO, RLVR).
  • Multimodal and agentic AI (Ch 20-30): vision-language, audio, video, 3D, and omni-models; agent foundations, tool use (function calling, MCP, A2A), multi-agent systems, specialized agents, and agent safety.
  • Retrieval and conversation (Ch 31-41): embeddings and vector databases, RAG, advanced and agentic RAG, information extraction, conversational design, and voice and real-time multimodal.
  • Evaluation, security, and governance (Ch 42-56): evaluation and observability, LLM-as-judge, prompt injection and guardrails, privacy, bias and fairness, watermarking, and regulation (EU AI Act).
  • Scale, LLMOps, and industries (Ch 57-74): frontier hardware, distributed training, serving infrastructure, production engineering, gateways and routing, reliability and SLOs, and domain systems for healthcare, finance, legal, education, cybersecurity, government, and manufacturing.
  • Research frontiers (Ch 75-78): state-space models and Mamba, mixture-of-experts, world models, open problems, and a 2026 retrospective.

Who this book is for

Software engineers, ML practitioners, researchers, domain specialists, and educators. Familiarity with Python is assumed; the Mathematical Foundations appendix covers the prerequisites for readers who need them.

What makes it different

  • Eight goal-based reading pathways (RAG engineers, agent builders, ML practitioners moving to LLMs, researchers, safety specialists, instructors, and generalists) so you read in the order that serves your goal.
  • Course-ready syllabi for undergraduate and graduate engineering and research tracks and professional bootcamps.
  • Built to teach: hundreds of hands-on labs (at least one per chapter), "under the hood" deep dives, worked derivations, diagrams, and production-pattern callouts.
  • Up to date for 2026: reasoning models (o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1), test-time compute, modern PEFT, agentic protocols (MCP, A2A, AG-UI), multimodal foundation models, FlashAttention-3, PagedAttention, and RLVR.
  • End-to-end capstone project: design, build, evaluate, and present a production-grade LLM application that integrates every major skill from the book.

About the authors

Alexander (Sasha) Apartsin, Ph.D. (Holon Institute of Technology) and Yehudit Aperstein, Ph.D. (Afeka Academic College of Engineering) are AI faculty with decades of combined experience teaching LLMs, agents, generative AI, NLP, computer vision, and deep learning.
