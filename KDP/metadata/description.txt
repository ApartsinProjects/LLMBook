BUILDING CONVERSATIONAL AI WITH LLMS AND AGENTS

A 79-chapter LLM textbook (chapters numbered 0 through 78, opening with a Chapter 0 PyTorch and classical-ML primer) that takes you from PyTorch fundamentals to shipping autonomous agents in production. Over 1,000 pages of theory, working code, and production engineering, organized into 15 parts plus 6 reference appendices, written for engineers, researchers, and students who need to build and operate real LLM systems. The Sixteenth Edition adds a new chapter on LLM-powered recommender systems (TIGER, LLaRA, P5), a complete audio and speech track (codecs, wav2vec, HuBERT, Whisper, CLAP), a PyTorch reference appendix, a signal-processing primer for audio, and full architecture diagrams plus math plus code plus worked examples for every load-bearing algorithm.

If you have spent the last year learning LLMs from scattered blog posts, GitHub READMEs, and outdated tutorials, this book is for you. Every chapter answers a specific question someone shipping LLM-powered systems has actually had to answer: how attention works, how to fine-tune a model on your data, how to build an agent that uses tools without going off the rails, how to evaluate the thing you just built, and how to run it reliably at scale.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN

Part I, LLM Building Blocks (Ch 0-5): PyTorch and classical-ML foundations, NLP and text representation, tokenization, sequence models and the attention mechanism, the Transformer architecture built from scratch, and decoding strategies.

Part II, Understanding LLMs (Ch 6-10): pretraining and scaling laws, the modern model landscape, reasoning and test-time compute, inference optimization, and interpretability.

Part III, Working with LLMs (Ch 11-14): provider APIs and structured output, prompt engineering, hybrid ML plus LLM systems, and the working tooling stack.

Part IV, LLM Training and Adaptation (Ch 15-19): synthetic data, fine-tuning, parameter-efficient methods (LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA), distillation and model merging, and alignment with RLHF, DPO, and RLVR.

Part V, Multimodal LLMs (Ch 20-25): vision-language models, audio and speech, video, 3D and neural scenes, and any-to-any omni-models.

Part VI, Agentic AI (Ch 26-30): agent foundations, tool use (function calling, MCP, A2A), multi-agent systems, specialized agents, and agent safety and autonomy.

Part VII, Retrieval and Information Extraction with LLMs (Ch 31-36): embeddings and vector databases, retrieval-augmented generation, advanced RAG, retrieval tools, cross-modal and agentic RAG, and information extraction.

Part VIII, Conversational AI with LLMs (Ch 37-41): conversational system design, voice and real-time multimodal interaction, and conversational-AI tooling.

Part IX, LLM Evaluation and Observability (Ch 42-46): evaluation foundations, specialized evaluation, LLM-as-judge, and online evaluation and observability.

Part X, LLM Security and Runtime Safety (Ch 47-51): prompt injection and jailbreaks, runtime guardrails, agent safety, and privacy and data protection.

Part XI, LLM Ethics, Trust and Governance (Ch 52-56): bias and fairness, watermarking and provenance, regulation (including the EU AI Act), and governance.

Part XII, LLM Systems at Scale (Ch 57-61): frontier systems and hardware, distributed training systems, and high-throughput serving infrastructure.

Part XIII, LLMOps Lifecycle (Ch 62-66): production engineering, AI gateways and routing, reliability and SLOs, and model registries.

Part XIV, Applications of LLMs Across Industries (Ch 67-74): domain-specific systems for healthcare, finance, legal, education, cybersecurity, government, and manufacturing.

Part XV, LLM and Agentic AI Research Frontiers (Ch 75-78): emerging architectures (state-space models, Mamba, mixture-of-experts), world models, open problems, and a 2026 retrospective on what the field settled and what remains open.

WHO THIS BOOK IS FOR

Software engineers, ML practitioners, researchers, domain specialists, and educators. Familiarity with Python is assumed; the Mathematical Foundations appendix covers the math and ML prerequisites for readers who need them.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

Eight goal-based reading pathways for RAG engineers, agent builders, ML practitioners moving to LLMs, researchers, safety specialists, instructors, and curious generalists, so you can read the book in the order that serves your goal.

Course-ready: ready-to-teach syllabi spanning undergraduate engineering, undergraduate research, graduate engineering, graduate research, and professional bootcamp tracks.

Built to teach, not just to tell: hundreds of hands-on labs (at least one per chapter), "under the hood" deep dives, worked derivations, diagrams, and production-pattern callouts throughout. Learn by building, not just by reading.

Appendices: Mathematical Foundations, Course Syllabi, Reading Pathways, and a roster of the AI agents that helped produce the book.

Up to date for 2026: reasoning models (o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1), test-time compute scaling, modern PEFT, agentic protocols (MCP, A2A, AG-UI), multimodal foundation models, FlashAttention-3, PagedAttention, RLVR, and the production patterns that 2026 settled.

End-to-end capstone project: design, build, evaluate, and present a production-grade LLM application that integrates every major skill from the book.
