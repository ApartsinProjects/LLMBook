Models

Section 6.4

Part I uses three families of small public models: encoder-only (BERT and DistilBERT), decoder-only (GPT-2), and encoder-decoder (T5). They are old by 2026 standards. That is the point. Each one is small enough to load on a laptop, well-documented enough that you can read the original paper alongside the checkpoint, and structurally simple enough that the Chapter 4 implementations match the modeling code one-to-one.

6.4.1 BERT and friends

BERT-base-uncased (110M params) and BERT-large-uncased (340M params) are the encoders introduced by Devlin et al. in "BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers" (2018). They use masked language modeling and next-sentence prediction. Use them in Chapter 1 for embedding-style analyses and in Chapter 4 to read a working encoder forward pass.

For laptop work, prefer DistilBERT (66M params, 60 percent of BERT's speed with 97 percent of GLUE performance per the DistilBERT paper) or ModernBERT (an Answer.AI / LightOn re-implementation from late 2024 with 8K context and FlashAttention support). RoBERTa (roberta-base) is BERT trained better, no architectural change, and is the right "modernized BERT" baseline if you need encoder embeddings in 2026.

6.4.2 GPT-2

GPT-2 (124M / 355M / 774M / 1.5B params) is the open-source decoder we lean on hardest. The original repository and Karpathy's nanoGPT rewrite are the reference implementations every later open model traces back to. Chapter 4 builds a from-scratch GPT-2 forward pass; Chapter 5 uses the pretrained 124M weights to study decoding. The model is small enough to fine-tune on a single 8 GB GPU but big enough to produce plausibly fluent text when sampled, which makes it the right size for teaching.

For slightly larger but still public alternatives, Pythia-1.4B from EleutherAI ships with intermediate training checkpoints, which makes it the standard for any analysis that needs to watch a model learn over time.

6.4.3 T5 and the encoder-decoder option

T5-base (220M params) and T5-small (60M) appear in Chapter 5 as the encoder-decoder counterpoint to GPT-2's decoder-only design. The T5 paper framed every NLP task as text-to-text, which set the template for instruction-tuned models a few years later. For machine translation labs, BART-large-cnn is the typical drop-in for summarization.

6.4.5 Why we use old models on purpose

You will not find Llama-4 or GPT-4o in this section. Frontier models live in Chapter 12's tools list. The reason is pedagogical: Part I teaches mechanics, and mechanics are clearest in models you can dissect. BERT and GPT-2 have public checkpoints, public training code (for GPT-2), well-understood failure modes, and small enough sizes that one forward pass fits in a debugger. That combination is rare in current frontier releases, where weights are gated, training data is opaque, or the architecture is a moving target across point releases.

Key Insight

The progression from BERT (2018) to Llama-4 (2026) is mostly engineering: more data, more parameters, better data curation, RoPE instead of learned positional embeddings, RMSNorm instead of LayerNorm, grouped-query attention, mixture-of-experts. The Transformer block you build in Chapter 4 maps to all of them. Once you can read GPT-2, you can read GPT-4-class architectures, with footnotes for each delta.
