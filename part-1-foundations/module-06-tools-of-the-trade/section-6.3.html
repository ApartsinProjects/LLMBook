Datasets & Benchmarks

Section 6.3

You will not pretrain a frontier model in Part I. You will, however, tokenize and probe small corpora, fine-tune toy GPT-2 variants, and evaluate classifiers. The datasets below are the ones that show up in every Part I lab. They are old, public, well-documented, and small enough to fit on a laptop or a single consumer GPU. Their job is to teach mechanics, not to produce a benchmark-topping model.

6.3.1 Pretraining-style corpora

For the language-modeling labs in Chapters 1, 2, and 5, you want a corpus that is large enough to be interesting but small enough to iterate on.

  • WikiText-103: 103 million tokens of well-formed English from verified Wikipedia articles. The canonical small-LM benchmark, with documented preprocessing.
  • Wikipedia dumps (via Wikimedia): the full English Wikipedia, regenerated from scratch every few months. Use the wikimedia/wikipedia Hub release for clean text without the messy markup.
  • BookCorpus: 11,000 ebooks, originally used to pretrain BERT. Still hosted on the Hub for reproducibility, although you should read the data card on licensing.
  • C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus): 750 GB of cleaned web text, the basis of T5 and many later models. The en.noblocklist subset is the most widely cited variant.
  • FineWeb: a 2024 web-scale corpus (15T tokens) that has largely superseded C4 for serious pretraining work. We mention it in Chapter 6 for completeness; Chapter 12 covers it properly because Part II is where you would actually use it.

6.3.2 Classification and downstream benchmarks

For the fine-tuning and probing labs (Chapters 1 and 4), the canonical benchmarks are still GLUE and SuperGLUE. These suites are dated (BERT-large already saturates several tasks), but they are the right teaching benchmark because they exercise a wide range of capabilities, fit in a few hundred megabytes, and have published baselines for every era of model.

  • GLUE: 9 English NLU tasks (sentiment, NLI, paraphrase, similarity). The original 2018 BERT paper's evaluation suite.
  • SuperGLUE: a harder follow-up suite released in 2019.
  • IMDB: 50K movie reviews; the canonical "hello world" of text classification.
  • SST-2: sentence-level sentiment, included in GLUE but useful as a standalone debugging dataset.

6.3.4 Loading patterns

One reason Part I exercises feel light is that datasets hides all the I/O. The streaming pattern in particular is what makes laptop-scale labs feasible. The Hugging Face course Chapter 5 walks through these patterns; the official streaming docs cover the edge cases (filtering, mapping, sharding) you will hit once labs grow up.

A note on data ethics

Every dataset in this section was scraped from text that human beings wrote, often without explicit consent. Part I uses them for teaching; production training raises licensing, copyright, and consent questions we revisit in Part IX. The data card linked from each Hub page is the right starting point for understanding what you are about to load.
