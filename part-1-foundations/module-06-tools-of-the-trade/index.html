Big Picture

Chapters 0 through 5 built the conceptual foundations: tensors, tokenization, sequence models, the Transformer, and decoding. This chapter is the practical companion. It lists the platforms you will install, the libraries you will import, the datasets you will load, the open-weights models you will probe, and the communities you will lurk in. None of these tools matter on their own. They matter because they let you reproduce a paper on a Tuesday afternoon and have something running by dinner.

Keep this chapter close. The next eleven Parts assume you have these basics installed and that you know roughly which library does what.

Library Shortcut

If you remember nothing else from Part I, remember these four lines. They install the foundation that every later chapter assumes:

pip install torch transformers tokenizers datasets

PyTorch gives you tensors and autograd; transformers gives you BERT, GPT-2, and a thousand other architectures behind a uniform API; tokenizers is the fast Rust-backed BPE/WordPiece encoder; datasets streams Wikipedia, C4, FineWeb, and most academic corpora with two lines of Python.

What Comes Next

Once your foundations stack is installed and verified, Part II turns to the modern LLM landscape: pre-training at scale, the model zoo from GPT-4o to Llama-4, reasoning systems, inference optimization, and interpretability.
