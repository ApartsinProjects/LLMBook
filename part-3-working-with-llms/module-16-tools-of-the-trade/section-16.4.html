Models

Section 16.4

The models you call in Part III split into three tiers by access mode. The first is closed-API frontier (GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro). The second is open-weight production tier (Llama-4, Qwen3, DeepSeek-V4) served either through an aggregator or through your own vLLM / Ollama instance. The third is the small fast tier (Mistral Small, Gemini Flash, Claude Haiku, GPT-5-mini) where latency and cost win over quality. This section walks all three and names the per-token cost order of magnitude as of mid-2026.

Treat the prices below as approximate. Provider pricing changes monthly and varies by region; Artificial Analysis is the right place to check current numbers. What stays roughly stable is the ratio: flagship models cost 10 to 50x what their small siblings cost, and self-hosted open-weight inference cost depends almost entirely on your hardware utilization.

16.4.1 Closed-API flagships

  • OpenAI GPT-5.5 (and reasoning variants): the GPT line. Strong on coding, native multimodal (vision + audio), Batch API for 50% discount, prompt caching, structured outputs.
  • Anthropic Claude Opus 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 4.6: strong on agentic tasks, computer use, extended thinking, prompt caching, 200K context default and 1M for select customers.
  • Google Gemini 3.1 Pro: top of Humanity's Last Exam, 1M-2M context, deeply multimodal (PDF, video, audio).

16.4.2 Closed-API small / fast tier

  • GPT-5-mini and GPT-5-nano: cheap fast tier; ~10x cheaper than 5.5, sometimes faster than 4.
  • Claude Haiku 4.5: smallest Claude; ~$1/$5 per million input/output tokens.
  • Gemini 2.5 Flash / Gemini 3 Flash: the cheapest of the frontier-family flash tier, often the best $/token in the market.
  • Mistral Small / Codestral: cheap-tier Mistral; OpenAI-compatible API.

16.4.3 Open-weight models served via API

  • OpenRouter aggregates Llama-4, Qwen3, DeepSeek-V4, Mistral Large, and dozens of fine-tunes under a single OpenAI-compatible key. Useful for cheap access without provisioning your own GPUs.
  • Together AI, Groq, Fireworks: dedicated open-weight inference providers. Groq's LPU offers the lowest latency on the market (under 200 ms time-to-first-token for many open models).
  • DeepInfra and Anyscale Endpoints: similar aggregators with different pricing tradeoffs.

The cheap-tier models cleared the bar for most real work in 2025

Through 2023 and most of 2024, the smallest models from each provider were obviously worse: they hallucinated more, refused more, missed instructions. By mid-2025 the gap closed: Gemini Flash, Claude Haiku, and GPT-5-mini handle most production tasks (extraction, classification, simple coding, RAG answer-generation) within 5% of their flagship siblings at 10 to 50x lower cost. Default to the cheap tier; reach for the flagship only when you measure a quality gap on your own task.

Practical example: prompt caching cuts repeated-context cost by 90%
import anthropic
client = anthropic.Anthropic()
SYSTEM = open("long-system-prompt.md").read()  # 50K tokens, reused

resp = client.messages.create(
    model="claude-opus-4-6",
    max_tokens=1024,
    system=[{"type":"text","text":SYSTEM,
             "cache_control":{"type":"ephemeral"}}],
    messages=[{"role":"user","content":"summarize section 3"}],
)

The cache_control flag tells Anthropic to cache the 50K-token system prompt for 5 minutes. Subsequent calls within the cache window pay 0.1x the input price for the cached portion. OpenAI implements automatic prompt caching (no flag needed); Gemini exposes Context Caching via an explicit cache resource. All three SDKs follow the same pattern and the savings are typically 70 to 90% for RAG pipelines.

16.4.5 Picking a default

For Part III exercises: start with Claude Haiku 4.5 or Gemini Flash for cheap calls, Claude Opus 4.6 or GPT-5.5 for the flagship comparisons, and DeepSeek-V4 through OpenRouter when you need a low-cost reasoning model. If you anticipate hosting your own inference at any scale, the next section (16.5) and Chapter 50's full Idea-to-Product Tools of the Trade chapter cover the cost-break math.
