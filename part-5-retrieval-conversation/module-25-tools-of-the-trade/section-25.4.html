Models

Section 25.4

Part V uses two model categories: embedding models (which encode chunks into vectors) and reranker models (which re-score candidates after first-stage retrieval). The LLMs that generate the final answer were covered in Chapter 12; this section focuses on the retrieval-specific models.

25.4.1 Open-source embedding models

  • BGE family (BAAI, 2023) includes bge-base, bge-large-en-v1.5 (the English production default) and bge-m3 (the multilingual + multi-functional successor). Its objective is to provide the open-source SOTA on MTEB and BEIR with MIT-licensed weights, which matters when you need top-tier retrieval quality without paying per-token API fees. The core concept across the family is multi-stage contrastive training with mined hard negatives; bge-m3 specifically supports dense, sparse, and ColBERT-style multi-vector retrieval from one model. Pick bge-large-en for English-only retrieval; pick bge-m3 when you need multilingual or hybrid dense+sparse search.
  • E5 family (Microsoft, 2022) covers e5-base, e5-large-v2, and the multilingual-e5 variants, trained with weakly-supervised contrastive pretraining. Its objective is to demonstrate that web-scale weak supervision (mined query-passage pairs from CCNet, Reddit, etc.) can match curated datasets, which matters when you want a model that generalizes off MS MARCO. The core concept is "Embeddings from billion-scale Pairs": train on diverse weak pairs first, then fine-tune on labeled data. Pick E5 as a strong alternative to BGE when you want a different training-data distribution for ensemble or A/B reasons.
  • Snowflake Arctic Embed (Snowflake, 2024) is Snowflake's open embedding family (xs through l), positioned for production retrieval inside their data warehouse. Its objective is to deliver top-MTEB quality with small enough sizes to be hosted economically, which matters when you have hundreds of millions of documents and embedding cost dominates. The core concept is heavy negative-mining and multi-stage curriculum training similar to BGE but with a slightly different blend. Pick Arctic Embed when you want a Snowflake-affiliated option or want to ensemble against BGE.
  • Nomic Embed (Nomic AI, 2024) is an open embedding model with first-class Matryoshka representations, letting you truncate the 768-dim vector to 64, 128, or 256 dims with graceful degradation. Its objective is to give self-hosted embeddings the flexibility OpenAI's text-embedding-3 introduced, which matters when your vector DB charges per dimension or you want to A/B test storage budgets. Pick Nomic Embed when dimension-flexibility matters and you want fully open weights; for raw quality, BGE-large still edges it on MTEB.
  • all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (UKP Lab, 2021) is the 22M-parameter "fast and good enough" embedding model that defaults in dozens of tutorials and starter codebases. Its objective is to provide reasonable retrieval quality with embedding latency under 10 ms on CPU, which matters for resource-constrained or edge deployments. The core concept is a heavily-distilled MiniLM trained on a billion sentence pairs. Pick MiniLM for prototypes, edge deployments, or as a fallback when GPU embedding is unavailable; for production retrieval quality, BGE-large is a clear upgrade.
  • NV-Embed-v2 (NVIDIA, 2024) topped MTEB in 2024; relevant when you can host a 7B-class embedding model and want a top-of-leaderboard open option.
  • SFR-Embedding-Mistral (Salesforce, 2024): another 7B-class embedder that traded the MTEB top spot through 2024.
  • stella_en_400M_v5 and stella_en_1.5B_v5: surprisingly strong tiny embedders punching far above their parameter count.
  • BGE-en-icl (BAAI, 2024) and Linq-Embed-Mistral: recent open releases worth flagging when picking 2026 embedding models.

Two architectural threads sit alongside single-vector dense embeddings and deserve their own awareness: Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2022, arXiv:2205.13147) is the trick that lets every modern embedding model truncate to fewer dimensions without retraining; OpenAI, Nomic, Voyage, and Gemini all implement it. Late-interaction retrieval (ColBERTv2, JaColBERTv2, ColPali for documents-with-figures) is the second axis of retrieval models, trading storage for higher recall via per-token vectors. Reasoning rerankers (using R1-style reasoning for relevance scoring) are emerging post-2025.

25.4.2 API-based embeddings

  • OpenAI text-embedding-3-large / -small (OpenAI, 2024) are the third-generation OpenAI embedding APIs with 3072 and 1536 dimensions respectively. Their objective is to be the easiest-to-integrate state-of-the-art embedding, which matters when you have low retrieval volume and want zero infrastructure. The core concept is Matryoshka representation: any of the dimensions are a valid prefix, so you can truncate 3-large to 1024 with minor quality loss. Pick text-embedding-3-large for top-quality API retrieval; pick -small when cost dominates and quality tradeoff is acceptable.
  • Cohere embed-multilingual-v4 (Cohere, 2025): the 2025 successor to Embed v3, still the off-the-shelf default for non-English RAG. The original v3 is still supported for cost-sensitive workloads but v4 is the current recommendation.
  • Voyage AI (Voyage AI, 2023) is Anthropic's officially-recommended embedding partner, distinguished by domain-specialized variants (voyage-code, voyage-law, voyage-finance). Its objective is to outperform generic embeddings on vertical applications via in-domain pretraining, which matters when generic embeddings under-perform on your specific document type. Pick Voyage when you have a clear vertical and want a managed embedding API that Anthropic publicly endorses.
  • Google Gemini Embedding (Google, 2024) is Google's API embedding model, distinguished by a 3072-dim native output and aggressive Matryoshka support. Its objective is to be the embedding companion to Gemini Pro for Vertex AI customers, which matters inside the Google Cloud ecosystem. Pick it when you are already on Vertex AI and want one-vendor billing; outside GCP, OpenAI and Cohere have more momentum.

25.4.3 Reranker models

  • BGE Reranker v2-m3 (BAAI, 2024) is BAAI's open multilingual cross-encoder reranker built on the same training stack as BGE embeddings. Its objective is to provide Cohere-Rerank-quality results with MIT-licensed open weights, which matters when you want a fully self-hosted RAG pipeline. The core concept is a cross-encoder that scores (query, document) jointly rather than embedding them separately, capturing fine-grained relevance signals embeddings miss. Pick bge-reranker as your open-source reranker default in 2026.
  • Cohere Rerank v3.5 (Cohere, 2025) is the 2025 update to the managed reranker API and the production default after several years of benchmarks showing 10-20 nDCG-point lifts on top of dense retrieval. Its objective is to add reranking with one HTTP call rather than hosting a cross-encoder, which matters when reranker volume is moderate (the API cost is reasonable up to single-digit millions of calls per day). Pick Cohere Rerank for managed simplicity; for high-volume self-hosting, bge-reranker is the substitute.
  • Jina Reranker v2 (Jina AI, 2024) is an open multilingual reranker positioned as a Cohere alternative for European deployments. Its objective is to provide a competitive reranker with EU-friendly hosting options, which matters when data-residency constraints rule out US-based APIs. Pick Jina Reranker when you need an open reranker with strong multilingual coverage and prefer Jina's hosting in EU regions.
  • mxbai-rerank (Mixedbread, 2024) is Mixedbread's open reranker family, holding the top spot on several public reranker benchmarks at release. Its objective is to demonstrate that small carefully-tuned open rerankers can beat the big proprietary ones on cost-adjusted metrics, which matters when reranker hosting is the dominant cost. Pick mxbai-rerank when benchmark performance per parameter is your priority; for production stability, bge-reranker has wider deployment coverage.

Reranking is the cheap win

The largest quality lift in most RAG pipelines comes not from a better embedding model but from adding a reranker. A two-stage system (cheap embedding -> top-100 -> rerank to top-5) routinely outperforms a single-stage system with the best available embeddings, at a small fraction of the cost.
