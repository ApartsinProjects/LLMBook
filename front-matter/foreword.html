Foreword

Why This Book Exists

Large language models went from research curiosity to production substrate in the span of three years. The pace caught practitioners on every side off guard. Engineers shipped agents into production faster than the patterns to evaluate them existed. Researchers published faster than the textbooks could absorb. Educators rebuilt syllabi from scratch every semester, often citing GitHub READMEs because the books were already out of date.

We wrote this book to bridge that gap between the breadth of the field and the depth a working practitioner needs. Each of the 35 chapters earns its place by answering a specific question someone shipping LLM-powered systems has actually had to answer: How does attention work? How do I fine-tune a model on my data? How do I build an agent that uses tools without going off the rails? How do I evaluate the thing I just built? We start with PyTorch fundamentals and end with the strategy for deciding which problems are even worth solving with LLMs, with everything in between.

This is a textbook, not a tutorial. It assumes you can write Python and that you have a working laptop. It does not assume you have prior LLM experience. The reference appendices cover everything else: framework cheat sheets (HuggingFace and LangChain), the math you might be rusty on, environment setup, and the production tooling we wish someone had told us about earlier.

How to Read It

You do not have to read this book in order. FM.3 What This Book Covers includes a chapter dependency diagram, and Appendix AJ: Reading Pathways offers eight goal-based routes (RAG engineer, agent builder, ML-to-LLM transitioner, product leader, instructor, and others). Pick the pathway that matches your situation and the book becomes a much shorter read.

If you find this book helpful, please leave an honest review on your retailer of choice. For independent academic work, reader reviews are the single most important signal that determines whether the book reaches the next reader.
