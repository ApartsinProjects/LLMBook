1. Every Chapter Has a Big Picture

Each chapter opens with a one-paragraph summary that tells you the conceptual point before any math. Here is one from Chapter 12 (Prompt Engineering):

Big Picture

Why does prompting matter? A large language model is a powerful text-completion engine, but its output is entirely shaped by its input. The same model that produces vague, rambling answers to a poorly worded question can deliver precise, structured, expert-level responses when given a well-designed prompt. Prompt engineering is the art and science of designing those inputs.

2. Every Algorithm Has a Why

We do not just present algorithms; we explain why they were designed the way they are. Example from Chapter 04 (The Transformer Architecture):

Key Insight: Why Scale by 1/√dk

The scaled dot-product attention formula divides by √dk. That divisor is not cosmetic; it prevents softmax from collapsing into a near-one-hot distribution. Without scaling, large dot products push softmax to assign all attention to a single token, killing learning. Scaling keeps the variance of attention scores stable across embedding dimensions.

3. Every Chapter Has Hands-On Labs

Practical Example: Build a RAG Pipeline in 50 Lines

Chapter 19 walks you through building a complete retrieval-augmented generation system: chunk a corpus, embed with sentence-transformers, store in a FAISS vector index, retrieve top-k passages for a query, and generate an answer with GPT-4o or Claude. By the end of the chapter you have a working RAG pipeline you can adapt to your own data.
