Building Language AIFrom Tokens to Agents

A practitioner's guide to large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning, and agentic systems.

✦
Alexander (Sasha) Apartsin, Ph.D. & Yehudit Aperstein, Ph.D.

Language is the interface through which intelligence becomes useful. This book is one connected journey through the ideas, models, and engineering that let machines understand, generate, and reason through language. It starts with tokens and the mathematics of attention, builds through the transformer and the training and adaptation of large language models, and their multimodal and retrieval-augmented extensions, then moves into autonomous agents that reason, use tools, and coordinate, before closing with the evaluation, safety, and governance concerns that govern real systems. A recurring thread bridges classical machine learning and modern LLMs, so you leave with one mental model rather than a catalogue of tricks.

What You Will Learn

One connected journey from tokens to autonomous agents. 79 chapters across 15 parts, plus 6 reference appendices, each idea built from first principles before the libraries that put it to work.

Part I · Building Blocks
PyTorch & Transformers from scratch
Six chapters take you from tensors and gradients through tokenization, attention, and a working transformer block. Decoding Strategies & Text Generation shows exactly how a next-token distribution becomes useful text.
Part II · Understanding LLMs
Scaling laws, reasoning, interpretability
Pretraining and Chinchilla-style scaling, the closed-vs-open landscape, reasoning models that "think longer" at inference, KV-cache and speculative decoding, plus mechanistic interpretability of what models actually learn.
Part III · Working with LLMs
APIs, prompts, hybrid ML+LLM design
Provider APIs as your primary interface, prompt engineering as a measurable craft, and decision frameworks for when a classical ML pipeline beats an LLM call (and when to combine them).
Part IV · Training & Adaptation
Synthetic data, LoRA, RLHF, DPO
Generate synthetic training data, run supervised fine-tuning, master Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning with LoRA and QLoRA, distill and merge models, then align with RLHF, RLAIF, and Direct Preference Optimization.
Part V · Multimodal LLMs
Vision, audio, video, 3D, robotics
CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP-3, and LLaVA for vision-language; TTS and music generation; 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF; plus VLA models like RT-2, OpenVLA, and pi-0 for LLM-powered robotics.
Part VI · Agentic AI
Tool use, MCP/A2A, multi-agent systems
Agent foundations and the ReAct loop, function calling and the MCP, A2A, and AG-UI protocols, coordination patterns for multi-agent systems, and specialized agents for coding, research, and data analysis.
Part VII · Retrieval & IE
Embeddings, RAG, knowledge graphs
Vector databases and semantic search, complete RAG pipelines, cross-modal and multimodal RAG, structured information extraction and NER, plus GraphRAG with knowledge-graph ingestion.
Part VIII · Conversational AI
Dialogue, memory, voice assistants
Build conversational systems that combine prompt engineering, memory, and retrieval; ship voice and realtime multimodal assistants over streaming audio and realtime APIs.
Part IX · Evaluation & Observability
LLM-as-judge, RAG & agent eval
Quality metrics that catch what unit tests miss, specialized evaluation for RAG faithfulness and agent trajectories, online observability with OpenTelemetry and distributed tracing, and reliable LLM-as-judge ensembles.
Part X · Security & Runtime Safety
Red-teaming, guardrails, privacy
Adversarial attacks and red-team playbooks, runtime guardrails and output filtering, agent-specific threat models with prompt-injection defenses, plus memorization attacks, differential privacy, and machine unlearning.
Part XI · Ethics & Governance
EU AI Act, watermarking, Green AI
Bias, fairness, and hallucination mitigation; EU AI Act, GDPR, and NIST AI RMF compliance-as-code; C2PA watermarking and provenance; model cards and audit trails; and carbon accounting with CodeCarbon.
Part XII · Systems at Scale
Distributed training, edge, frontier silicon
Compute planning and infrastructure sizing, distributed training systems, non-NVIDIA silicon and decentralized training, and edge / on-device LLM deployment.
Part XIII · LLMOps
AI gateways, Kubernetes, SLOs
Production engineering for LLM systems: AI gateways and model routing, durable workflow orchestration, Kubernetes-native deployment, reliability with SLOs, and a working model registry.
Part XIV · Industry Applications
Legal, finance, healthcare, gov, security
Eight industry deep-dives: contract review and e-discovery; research and compliance in finance; ambient documentation and FDA / HIPAA realities in healthcare; tutoring and assessment; SOC automation; FOIA and benefits; plus manufacturing and creative.
Part XV · Research Frontiers
Emergent abilities, AGI trajectories
Frontier architectures and scaling, formal theories of reasoning and agency, mechanistic interpretability at scale, and the timelines, benchmarks, and alignment debates that define AGI trajectories.
Appendices A – G
6 reference appendices
Mathematical foundations, course syllabi, reading pathways, the agent roster, a PyTorch reference, and signal processing for audio.
Capstone Project
End-to-end LLM system (4-6 weeks)
Design, build, evaluate, and present a production-grade LLM application that integrates every major skill from the book. Start here →

How This Book Teaches

One connected story, not a reference dump. Every technique is built from first principles, then wired to the production library you would actually reach for.

From Scratch, Then Frameworks
Build it by hand first
Attention, a transformer block, a RAG pipeline, an agent loop: you implement the core before reaching for PyTorch, Hugging Face, or LangChain, so the abstractions never feel like magic.
Worked Pipelines
Runnable end to end
Code appears as complete, runnable pipelines rather than disconnected snippets, with the inputs, outputs, and failure modes you actually hit in production.
A Callout System
Definitions, pitfalls, production notes
A consistent set of callouts flags formal definitions, common pitfalls, and "in production" caveats, so you can skim for exactly the layer you need.
Exercises & Labs
Practice every chapter
Each chapter closes with exercises and hands-on labs that turn the reading into working code and measurable results.
Reading Pathways
Eight goal-based routes
Eight goal-based reading pathways and five university course syllabi route you to the chapters that matter for your goal.
Classical ML Returns
When not to use an LLM
Decision frameworks show where a classical ML pipeline still beats an LLM call, and how to combine the two, so you build the right system rather than the trendy one.
