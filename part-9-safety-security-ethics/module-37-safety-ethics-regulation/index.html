Safety, Ethics & Regulation

"With great power comes great responsibility. The same technology that can democratize access to knowledge can also amplify harm at unprecedented scale."

Sage, Morally Conflicted AI Agent
Looking Back

Parts III-VIII built and operated LLM systems. Part IX zooms out to the questions that determine whether those systems are allowed to exist: safety threats, hallucination defense, bias and fairness, regulation (the EU AI Act in particular), licensing and IP, machine unlearning, red-teaming frameworks, environmental impact, and privacy attacks. The compliance chapter is the one you read before launch, not after.

Chapter Overview

With the production engineering foundations from Chapter 35 in place, this chapter tackles the safety, ethical, and regulatory dimensions of deploying LLMs at scale. It covers the OWASP Top 10 for LLMs, prompt injection defenses, hallucination detection and mitigation, bias measurement, model cards, and environmental impact.

Building on the alignment techniques covered in Chapter 20, the regulatory landscape (EU AI Act, GDPR, US executive orders) and enterprise governance frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001) are examined alongside practical audit strategies. The chapter also covers red teaming frameworks and automated security testing (PyRIT, Garak, HarmBench), EU AI Act compliance in practice, environmental impact and Green AI, privacy attacks and differential privacy defenses, and federated learning for privacy-preserving LLM training. It concludes with licensing, intellectual property, and machine unlearning, preparing the ground for the strategic and ROI considerations in Chapter 31.

Big Picture

As LLMs become embedded in high-stakes decisions, safety and ethics move from nice-to-have to regulatory requirements. This chapter covers bias detection, content filtering, red-teaming, and emerging AI regulations. It builds on the alignment techniques of Chapter 20 and applies to every system deployed in production.

Learning Objectives
  • Defend against OWASP Top 10 LLM threats including prompt injection, jailbreaking, and data exfiltration
  • Detect and mitigate hallucinations using self-consistency, citation verification, and constrained generation, complementing interpretability methods from Chapter 11
  • Measure and reduce bias in LLM outputs through systematic auditing and model cards
  • Navigate the EU AI Act, GDPR, and US regulatory frameworks for AI governance
  • Implement enterprise risk governance using NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, and SR 11-7 frameworks
  • Understand model licensing taxonomies, IP ownership, and differential privacy for LLM training data
  • Apply machine unlearning techniques for GDPR compliance, copyright removal, and safety alignment
  • Conduct structured red teaming using PyRIT, Garak, and adversarial prompt libraries with CI/CD integration
  • Implement EU AI Act compliance for GPAI models, including risk classification and conformity assessment
  • Use automated red teaming benchmarks (HarmBench, JailbreakBench) for reproducible security evaluation
  • Assess and reduce the environmental impact of LLM training using carbon tracking and efficiency techniques
  • Defend against privacy attacks (training data extraction, membership inference) using differential privacy and defense-in-depth strategies
  • Design federated learning systems for LLMs using FedAvg, federated LoRA, and secure aggregation frameworks

Prerequisites

  • Chapter 35: Production Engineering and Operations (deployment, guardrails, LLMOps)
  • Chapter 14: Prompt Engineering (prompt design, structured outputs)
  • Chapter 34: Evaluation and Observability (metrics, tracing, monitoring)
  • Chapter 20: Alignment, RLHF, and DPO (alignment techniques)

Sections

  • 37.1 LLM Security Threats OWASP Top 10 for LLMs, prompt injection, jailbreaking defenses, input sanitization, sandwich defense, output scanning, and PII redaction strategies.
  • 37.2 Why LLMs Hallucinate and How to Catch Them Types of hallucination, detection methods (self-consistency, citation verification, NLI), mitigation strategies (RAG, constrained generation, confidence calibration), and abstention.
  • 37.3 Bias, Fairness & Ethics Sources of bias in LLMs, measurement techniques, fairness metrics, model cards, datasheets for datasets, and environmental impact of large-scale training.
  • 37.4 Regulation & Compliance EU AI Act risk tiers, GDPR requirements for AI, US executive orders, sector-specific regulations (HIPAA, finance, education), and building AI governance programs.
  • 37.5 LLM Risk Governance & Audit Enterprise model inventory, risk classification frameworks, SR 11-7 model risk management, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, and audit trail implementation.
  • 37.6 LLM Licensing, IP & Privacy Model license families (proprietary, open-weight, restricted-use), copyright in training data and generated output, PII handling, and the contractual surface that determines where an LLM application is allowed to operate.
  • 37.7 Machine Unlearning Removing specific training data influence from a deployed model. Approximate unlearning techniques, certified unlearning bounds, and the right-to-be-forgotten compliance angle.
  • 37.8 Red Teaming Frameworks & LLM Security Testing Structured adversarial probing of deployed models. Attack taxonomies, automated red-team tooling (Garak, PyRIT), and integrating red-team findings into the pre-deployment quality gate.
  • 37.9 EU AI Act Compliance in Practice Translating the EU AI Act's risk tiers into concrete engineering deliverables: conformity assessments, technical documentation, post-market monitoring, and what each tier requires of a system before it can be placed on the EU market.
  • 37.10 Environmental Impact & Green AI Carbon and water footprint of training and inference. Measurement methodology, efficient-model design, and the public reporting practices that have started showing up in vendor sustainability disclosures.
  • 37.11 Privacy Attacks & Differential Privacy for LLMs Membership inference, training-data extraction, model inversion. Differential privacy during training, DP-SGD, and the practical trade-offs between privacy budget and model utility for LLM workloads.
  • 37.12 AI Governance and Open Problems The unresolved policy and research questions: dual-use risk, international coordination, frontier-model evaluations, and the governance gaps that the next few years of regulation will have to close.
