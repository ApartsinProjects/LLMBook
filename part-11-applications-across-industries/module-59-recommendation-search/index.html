LLM-Powered Recommendation & Search

Ranking, retrieval, and personalization at scale.

Big Picture

Recommendation and search are the largest deployed application of machine learning in the world. Every major consumer internet platform lives or dies by the quality of its ranking, and these systems collectively serve trillions of recommendations per day. The 2022 to 2026 wave of LLMs and modern embedding models has reshaped this stack in three specific ways: richer semantic embeddings replace earlier ID-based two-tower architectures, cold-start and long-tail items are interpreted by language models rather than requiring extensive user interaction data, and users now query in natural language rather than only by clicking. This chapter walks through how production teams at Spotify, Pinterest, Netflix, and YouTube have integrated LLMs into the multi-stage recommendation funnel, what changed, and what stayed the same.

Real-World Scenario: Pinterest Visual Search at a Billion-Item Scale

Pinterest's visual search feature, "Lens," lets users photograph an object and find similar pins from a catalog of more than five billion images. The technical backbone is a CLIP-style dual encoder: the user's photo and every Pin in the catalog are mapped into a shared embedding space, and approximate nearest neighbor search retrieves the most similar Pins in tens of milliseconds. Pinterest's engineering team has been public about scaling this stack across visual search, related-Pin recommendations, and ad targeting, all sharing one embedding space. The lesson: unifying embeddings across surfaces (rather than training separate models per use case) reduces both training cost and quality variance. Spotify's Daylist applies the same architectural principle in audio: shared audio embeddings power similar-track recommendations, playlist seeding, and personalized playlist titles generated by an LLM on top.
