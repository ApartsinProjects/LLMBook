Appendix R: 2026 Freshness Index

What this page is

An index of the 2024–2026 papers, models, protocols, and benchmarks that define the field at the publication date of this edition. Use it as (1) a quick way to verify the book is current when you pick it up, (2) a reading list for catching up on the year before publication, and (3) the canonical citation list when chapters reference these results.

Architecture results added or updated

  • FlashAttention-3 (Shah et al. 2024): exploits H100 asynchronous memory ops + FP8 to reach 740 TFLOPS vs FA-2's 330 TFLOPS on the same hardware. Updates the “2–4× speedup” FA-2 era number in Section 4.4 to current H100-class numbers.
  • Multi-token prediction (Gloeckle et al. 2024, “Better and Faster Large Language Models via Multi-Token Prediction”): predicting the next 4 tokens simultaneously improves code-reasoning by ~17% at no inference-cost increase. Genuine training-paradigm shift; section in Ch 6 + Ch 33.
  • Mamba and SSM revival (Gu & Dao 2023, Lieber et al. 2024 Jamba): linear-time alternative to attention; section in Ch 33.
  • Prefill/decode disaggregation (Mooncake, Splitwise): two-cluster serving model. Production standard for high-throughput LLM services in 2026. Section in Ch 9.
  • YaRN long-context extension: standard for extending RoPE-based context windows beyond pretraining length. Section in Ch 4.3.

Reasoning & alignment results

  • Olsson et al. 2022, “In-Context Learning and Induction Heads” (Anthropic Transformer Circuits): mechanistic explanation of why few-shot prompting works. Section 14.1 cross-references; Ch 10 develops fully.
  • Lightman et al. 2023, “Let's Verify Step by Step” (arXiv:2305.20050): Process Reward Models (PRMs) over outcome reward models. Foundational to o1/o3 training. Ch 17.4 cross-reference.
  • Gao et al. 2022, “Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization” (arXiv:2210.10760): the optimal KL coefficient depends on RM size. Quantifies reward hacking. Ch 17.1.
  • Sardana & Frankle 2023, “Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal” (arXiv:2401.00448): inference-aware scaling. Llama 3 deliberately violated Chinchilla because of inference economics. Ch 6.3.
  • Setlur et al. 2024, “Rewarding Progress”: PRM faithfulness can fail (model produces plausible-looking steps that don't causally contribute to the right answer). Ch 8.
  • Burns et al. 2023, “Weak-to-Strong Generalization” (arXiv:2312.09390): can a weak supervisor align a strong model? Foundational for the alignment-verification gap. Ch 10.
  • Bowman et al. 2022, “Measuring Progress on Scalable Oversight” (arXiv:2211.03540): formalizes the problem. Ch 10.
  • Casper et al. 2023, “Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of RLHF” (arXiv:2307.15217): the canonical critique of RLHF. Ch 17.

Retrieval & long-context results

  • Liu et al. 2023, “Lost in the Middle” (arXiv:2307.03172): models perform best when relevant info is at the start or end of context, with up to 40% accuracy drop in the middle. Quantifies why long-context is not a free lunch. Ch 19.
  • Edge et al. 2024, “From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach”: GraphRAG. Ch 19 RAG architecture comparison.
  • Asai et al. 2023, Self-RAG: model decides when to retrieve and how to use retrieved docs. Ch 19.
  • Anthropic Contextual Retrieval (2024): prepending document context to chunks before embedding to improve retrieval recall.
  • Late chunking (Jina 2024): embedding the full document then chunking, instead of the reverse, for better cross-chunk coherence.

Interpretability results

  • Elhage et al. 2021, “A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits” (Anthropic): foundational paper for mechanistic interpretability. Ch 10.
  • Templeton et al. 2024, “Scaling Monosemanticity” (Anthropic): sparse autoencoders find interpretable features in Claude 3 Sonnet. Major operational milestone for the alignment-verification gap. Ch 10.
  • Marks & Tegmark 2023, “The Geometry of Truth”: linear representations of truth values in LLMs.

Evaluation methodology results

  • Golchin & Surdeanu 2023; Yang et al. 2023; Oren et al. 2024: evidence that widely-used benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) are likely contaminated. Ch 28.
  • Kadavath et al. 2022, “Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know” + Xiong et al. 2024: calibration as a first-class evaluation concern. Ch 28.
  • TAU-bench, SWE-bench Verified, LiveBench: contamination-resistant agent and reasoning benchmarks. Ch 21 + Ch 28.

Synthetic data results

  • DeepSeek-R1 4-stage pipeline (DeepSeek 2025): cold-start SFT → large-scale RLVR → rejection-sampling SFT on RL outputs → final DPO/RLHF. Only public recipe for o1-class reasoning models. Ch 14 + Ch 17.
  • Microsoft Persona Hub (2024): 1B-AI-persona prompt sampling for synthetic data diversity. Ch 14.
  • FineWeb-Edu: curriculum filtering used in Phi-4 training. Ch 14.

Production engineering results

  • vLLM, SGLang, TGI, TensorRT-LLM: serving engines. Section 10.4.
  • Mooncake (ByteDance), Splitwise (Microsoft): prefill/decode disaggregation reference architectures.
  • Anthropic + Google prompt caching: 90%+ cost reduction on repeated long-context. Section 9.
  • Braintrust, Langfuse, Helicone, PromptLayer: production prompt-registry / eval / observability platforms. Section 28.
How to use the freshness index

If you are reading this book more than 12 months after the publication date (2026-05-13) and a chapter cites a paper or model not on this list, that chapter may be due for an update. Check the companion site at llmbook.apartsin.com for the latest version.
